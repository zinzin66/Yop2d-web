The Blueprint screen: every button explained

Beginner guide · no code

Français · English · Español · Deutsch · Italiano · Português · Русский · 中文 · 日本語

The Blueprint is the screen where you connect nodes to tell your game what to do. This guide does not teach you how to make a game: it explains what each button and each part of the screen is for. Open it from the editor with the Blueprint button.

The top bar

From left to right:

  • Back to the editor
    Leaves the Blueprint and returns to your scene. Your work is saved automatically, but you can tap 💾 before leaving.
  • Save
    Keeps your nodes in the project.
  • Reload the last save
    Goes back to the last saved version. Careful: anything you changed since then is lost.
  • Zoom out
    See more nodes, smaller. You can also pinch the screen with two fingers.
  • Normal zoom
    Sets the zoom back to 100%.
  • Zoom in
    See the nodes bigger.
  • Move only
    When it is on (blue), touching a node only moves it: you can no longer break a wire or open its settings by mistake. Handy when nodes are close together.
  • Select several nodes
    Drag your finger over empty space to circle nodes (or tap them one by one). A bar appears at the bottom to copy or delete them. You can also move them all together.
  • Paste
    Pastes the copied nodes, even in another scene or another project. Drag to place them, then tap “Place”.
  • Undo
    Goes back (50 steps).
  • Redo
    Puts back what you just undid.
  • Tidy up the nodes
    Arranges everything automatically: each event becomes a row from left to right. You can go back with ↶.
  • Delete the chosen node
    Tap a node first to choose it, then this button.
  • Duplicate the chosen node
    Makes a copy of the node, with its settings.
  • Fold / unfold the chosen node
    Shrinks the node to its title ([...]) to save space. Tap the button again to reopen it.
  • Read the Blueprint as text
    Shows your nodes as readable text, to re-read what your game does.
  • Import a script
    Adds or replaces nodes from a text (the { } format). For advanced users.
  • Story map
    For dialogue games: an overview of the story parts, choices and endings.
  • Help
    Opens the list of all nodes, with an explanation and an example for each.
👉 On a small screen the bar scrolls: drag it to see the last buttons. If your project has several scenes, a button with the scene's name appears next to the title: use it to switch scenes without leaving the Blueprint.

The screen at a glance

  1. 1
    Fold the list of nodes to get more room.
  2. 2
    Recent: the last nodes you used, to find them quickly.
  3. 3
    Categories: tap a category to open its list of nodes, then tap a node to place it on the screen.
  4. 4
    A node: a block that does one thing. Starting nodes like On Start are events: without them, nothing starts.
  5. 5
    The dots: the input (Enter) on the left, the output (Next) on the right. Connect an output to an input to say “then, do this”.
  6. 6
    The node's frame: it sums up its settings (which object, which value). Tap it to change them.
  7. 7
    A wire: it shows the order in which nodes run. A Condition has several outputs: True, False and Then (what follows, after either one).
  8. 8
    Dropping a wire on empty space opens a list of nodes: the one you choose is placed and connected right away.
👉 To find out what a node does, tap the ? button in the top bar: the list of all nodes opens, explained one by one.

A node's settings window

When you tap a node's frame (or when you place a node), its settings window opens. Here is the one for the Wait node.

  1. 1
    The node's name and, below it, a sentence saying what it does.
  2. 2
    Close: closes the window. Your changes are kept.
  3. 3
    The list on the left: ready-made pieces to put into your value, in groups that fold: Finger and screen, Objects (Insertion) (an object of the scene and its settings, like its position), the variables, the tags, Joystick and Time and game. Each line shows a clear name, then the short code (like doigt.x). Tap it: it is written into the field, at the cursor.
  4. 4
    Settings: all of the node's settings, as rows. If the node targets an object, a On what? part appears above to choose which one.
  5. 5
    A setting row: the name on the left (here the duration in seconds), the value on the right. Tap the row to change it.
  6. 6
    The “?”: a help bubble that explains the setting and offers ready-made values to tap.
  7. 7
    The pencil: opens the writing area under the row. For other settings you will see an arrow (choose from a list) or Yes / No.
  8. 8
    The chain: instead of typing a value, plug in a green node that works it out (a random number, an object's position, a variable…).
  9. 9
    The writing field: type the value here (a number, or a small calculation).
  10. 10
    The result: the final value obtained (here “= 1”). When useful, an “In plain words” line explains the formula.
  11. 11
    The keypad: digits and signs (+ − × ÷ …), SPACE and the eraser, to type without the phone's keyboard.
  12. 12
    Compare: opens the comparison signs (> < ≥ ≤ = ≠) and the words and / or / not / yes / no, for conditions.
