How do I animate a character from a single picture?

Beginner guide · no code

Français · English · Español · Deutsch · Italiano · Português · Русский · 中文 · 日本語

You have a single picture of your character, seen from the side? Yop2D cuts it into pieces (head, arms, legs…) and moves them to make the Idle, Walk, Run, Jump, Attack and Hit animations. You don't have to draw anything else, and you don't need to prepare a sprite sheet.

You need:
  • a project open in the editor;
  • a picture of your character seen from the side, preferably on a transparent background (feet on the ground, arms and legs clearly visible).

Step 1: open the workshop

  1. Open the Assets window (Resources panel).
  2. At the top, tap the Character button, then Choose the character picture.
  3. The character must face right to work. If it faces left, tap Flip the picture (mirror): your final pictures will still face left.
👉 The ↶ and ↷ buttons at the top undo and redo almost everything (up to 60 actions): try freely.

Step 2: place the skeleton

  1. Tap Place the skeleton for me: Yop2D tries to guess where the joints are.
  2. Then fix it: drag each circle onto the real joint (neck, shoulder, elbow, hand, hip, knees, ankles, toes). A magnifier shows the spot under your finger.
  3. Tap a circle to read, under its name, where it should go.
👉 Put the circles on the axis of the joint (the point the arm or leg turns around), not on its edge. The more accurate they are, the nicer the movement.

Step 3: outline each part

  1. Tap the Outlines step, then a part in the list or directly on the picture.
  2. Drag its points to follow the drawing's outline. A small + in the middle of a side adds a point; Remove the chosen point removes one.
  3. For an outline that is already well placed, try Automatic outline.
👉 Arms and legs are cut into several pieces (arm and forearm, thigh, shin and foot): that is what lets the elbow and knee bend.

Step 4: fill the gaps (optional)

When an arm or a leg moves, it leaves a gap where it was. The Stamp step shows the character in motion so you can see those gaps. Always start with 1. Pick the source area: tap a clean spot of the drawing, for example the clothing.

  • 2. Paint over the hole: Yop2D copies the chosen area onto the hole, like a stamp.
  • 2b. Paint with the area's color: the brush paints with one flat color, the one of the chosen area. Handy for a tear.
  • 2c. Fill by placing points: place points all around the hole, then tap Fill the area. You can move a point before lifting your finger.
  • 3. Eraser (remove hanging bits): erases what sticks out or hangs. The slider sets the size of the brush and the eraser.
  • Undo last stroke and Clear all let you start over.
👉 If the color spills over, go over it with the eraser: you can always fix it.

Step 5: choose the movement and try it

  1. In the Animate step, choose a movement: Idle, Walk, Run, Jump, Attack or Hit. You see it moving in a loop.
  2. Set the speed (frames per second) and the movement size (for smaller or bigger steps).

Step 6: create the pictures

  1. In the Create step, give your character a name and tick the movements to make.
  2. Tap Create the pictures. They are saved in your project's Images folder, and the animations are added to the list.
  3. To use them, place your character in the scene and give it the "platformer character" behavior: it recognizes the idle, walk, run and jump animations by itself. You can also play them with “Play animation”.
👉 The animation names (idle, walk, jump…) are the ones Yop2D's behaviors recognize: you have nothing to set.

If it doesn't work

  • The character faces the wrong way: tap "Flip the picture (mirror)" in step 1.
  • A limb has a gap when it moves: go to the Stamp step and fill it (source area, color or fill).
  • You see two arms: untick "This part stays on the body" in the list of parts.
  • The result looks damaged: use ↶ to go back, or place the skeleton points more precisely.
  • The drawing is seen from the front: for now, the workshop works on a character seen from the side.
